Bitcoin Addresses: How to Receive Bitcoin

If someone wants to send you bitcoin, they need a destination. In Bitcoin, this is usually a Bitcoin address.

Addresses make it possible to receive bitcoin without sharing your private keys.

Let's understand how addresses work, where they come from and how to use them safely.

1. What is a Bitcoin address?

A Bitcoin address is an identifier that can be used as a destination for a Bitcoin payment.

Your wallet creates addresses using cryptographic information derived from your wallet's keys.

Example of a Bitcoin address: bc1qexampleaddress000000000000000000000000

This is an invented example for learning. It is not a real address and must not be used to send bitcoin.

A Bitcoin address is not your private key. You can share a receiving address with someone who wants to pay you.

2. How does a wallet create an address?

A Bitcoin wallet uses cryptographic keys and mathematical operations to generate addresses.

A simplified explanation is:

  1. Your wallet creates or restores the necessary private keys.
  2. It derives corresponding public keys.
  3. It uses the appropriate Bitcoin address rules to produce an address.
  4. You can share that address to receive bitcoin.

Important distinction

Your private key must remain secret. Your receiving address can be shared.

Never send your recovery phrase or private key to someone who wants to pay you.

3. Different types of Bitcoin addresses

Bitcoin has several commonly used address formats. They can look different even though they are all used to receive bitcoin.

Legacy addresses — start with 1

These are older Bitcoin addresses using the original Pay-to-Public-Key-Hash format.

Example format:

1ExampleAddressNotForPayment123

P2SH addresses — start with 3

These addresses are used for Pay-to-Script-Hash outputs. They are also commonly associated with wrapped SegWit and other scripts.

Example format:

3ExampleAddressNotForPayment123

Native SegWit addresses — start with bc1q

These use the Bech32 format and are commonly used for native SegWit payments.

Example format:

bc1qexampleaddressnotforpayment

Taproot addresses — start with bc1p

These use the Bech32m format and support Pay-to-Taproot outputs.

Example format:

bc1pexampleaddressnotforpayment
Remember: These are illustrative examples, not real receiving addresses. Always copy an address directly from your own wallet.

4. What is a Bitcoin QR code?

A QR code is a visual way to represent information such as a Bitcoin address.

Instead of typing a long address manually, someone can scan your wallet's QR code with a compatible wallet application.

Example:

Alice wants to receive bitcoin from Bob. She opens the Receive section of her wallet and displays her Bitcoin QR code.

Bob scans it, checks the address and amount, and prepares his payment.

A QR code makes copying easier, but it does not guarantee that the payment details are correct. Always check the address and payment information.

5. Why can a wallet create new addresses?

Many Bitcoin wallets can generate multiple receiving addresses from the same wallet backup.

Using a different address for different payments can make it harder for outside observers to link all your payments together.

This is one way Bitcoin wallets can help improve financial privacy.

Example:

Alice receives one payment using Address A. Later, she receives another payment using Address B.

Both addresses may belong to the same wallet, but an outside observer may not immediately know that they are controlled by the same person.

Address reuse is possible, but using a fresh receiving address when appropriate generally improves privacy.

6. How to receive bitcoin safely

  1. Open your Bitcoin wallet.
  2. Select the Receive option.
  3. Choose the correct Bitcoin network.
  4. Copy the receiving address or display its QR code.
  5. Share the receiving information with the sender.
  6. Check your wallet or your own node to verify the payment.

Bitcoin transactions are generally irreversible once confirmed. Take care when giving someone a receiving address.

7. How to protect yourself from address replacement

Some malware can monitor your computer's clipboard. When you copy a Bitcoin address, the malware may replace it with another address controlled by an attacker.

Before sending bitcoin

  • Verify the receiving address using a trusted source.
  • Check the full address, not just its first and last characters.
  • Verify the address on your hardware wallet screen when possible.
  • Make sure the amount is correct.
  • For a large payment, consider sending a small test payment first.

8. Can you use a Bitcoin address to spend bitcoin?

No. A receiving address is not enough to spend bitcoin.

Spending requires a valid transaction authorised by the appropriate private key or spending conditions.

This is why you should never confuse a public receiving address with a private key.
