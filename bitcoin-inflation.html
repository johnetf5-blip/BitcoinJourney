Bitcoin and Inflation: Why Does Money Lose Purchasing Power?

Have you noticed that the same amount of money sometimes buys fewer things than it did years ago?

This lesson explains inflation, purchasing power, money creation and how Bitcoin's monetary system is different.

1. What is inflation?

Inflation is a general increase in the prices of goods and services over time.

When prices rise, each unit of money generally buys fewer goods and services. This means the purchasing power of money falls.

Simple example

Imagine that a shopping basket costs £50 today. If the general price level rises, the same basket might cost £55 later.

Your £50 has not changed as a number, but it buys less.

Inflation is usually measured using a basket of goods and services. In the UK, the Consumer Prices Index (CPI) is one important measure.

2. What is purchasing power?

Purchasing power describes how much you can buy with a certain amount of money.

If your income stays the same while prices rise, you may be able to afford fewer things.

Example: Your £100
Situation Price of an item What £100 buys
Before £10 10 items
After £12.50 8 items

In this example, the same £100 buys two fewer items.

3. Why do prices rise?

Inflation can happen for several reasons. It is not always caused by one single factor.

  • Demand: When demand for products and services grows faster than supply, businesses may raise prices.
  • Production costs: Higher energy, transport, wages or material costs can lead to higher prices.
  • Supply problems: Shortages or disruptions can make goods more expensive.
  • Money and credit: Changes in money and credit conditions can influence total demand and prices.

These causes can interact. An increase in the money supply does not automatically produce an immediate, proportional increase in consumer prices.

4. How is money created in modern banking?

Modern money includes cash and bank deposits. Bank deposits are the electronic balances people commonly use to make payments.

Commercial banks create new deposit money when they make loans. When a bank issues a loan, it generally records a loan asset and creates a corresponding deposit for the borrower.

Central banks influence monetary and financial conditions through tools such as interest rates and other monetary policy operations.

This does not mean banks can create unlimited money without constraints. Lending is affected by regulation, capital, funding, risk, demand for loans and other conditions.

5. What is monetary inflation?

The phrase monetary inflation can refer to an increase in the quantity of money in an economy.

Consumer price inflation is different: it describes the rise in prices paid for goods and services.

The two can be connected, but they are not identical. The effect of changes in money on prices depends on many economic factors, including production, demand and how quickly money circulates.

6. How is Bitcoin different?

Bitcoin has a published issuance schedule built into its protocol. New bitcoin is introduced through the block subsidy paid to miners when valid blocks are produced.

The subsidy is reduced by half approximately every 210,000 blocks. This event is called the halving.

Bitcoin's maximum supply is approximately 21 million BTC. Its issuance rules are checked by full nodes.

The subsidy continues to decrease over time. Eventually, new issuance through the block subsidy becomes zero, and miners can earn revenue from transaction fees.

7. Does Bitcoin guarantee protection from inflation?

Bitcoin's supply schedule is different from that of national currencies. Its maximum supply is part of its protocol rules.

However, a limited supply does not guarantee that Bitcoin's market price will rise or that its purchasing power will always increase.

Bitcoin's market price can be volatile. Its purchasing power changes depending on what people are willing to exchange for it.

Understanding the difference between a fixed issuance schedule and market value is important before making financial decisions.
