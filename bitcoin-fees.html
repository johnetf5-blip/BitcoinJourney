BITCOIN EDUCATION — LESSON 11

Bitcoin Transaction Fees: Why Do Transactions Cost Money?

When you send bitcoin, you usually pay a transaction fee.

But why is there a fee? Who receives it? Why can the same type of payment cost different amounts at different times?

In this lesson, we will understand Bitcoin fees and learn how to read a fee rate.

1. What is a Bitcoin transaction fee?

A Bitcoin transaction fee is an amount of bitcoin paid by the person creating a transaction to incentivise miners to include that transaction in a block.

The fee is not a payment to a bank or a central company. It is collected by the miner who successfully creates the block containing the transaction.

Simple example

Alice wants to send Bob 0.01 BTC. She creates a transaction and chooses a fee.

The transaction fee is separate from the 0.01 BTC she wants Bob to receive.

2. Why do Bitcoin transactions need fees?

Bitcoin blocks have limited space. Not every waiting transaction can be included in the next block.

Transaction fees create an incentive for miners to include transactions. They also help allocate limited block space when demand is high.

Fees are an important part of Bitcoin's economic design.

3. Who receives the transaction fees?

Miners collect the fees from the transactions included in the blocks they successfully mine.

A miner's block reward has two main parts:

  • Block subsidy: Newly issued bitcoin allowed by the protocol.
  • Transaction fees: Fees paid by users whose transactions are included in the block.

Remember

Bitcoin miners do not simply receive every fee in the network. A miner receives the fees from the transactions included in its successfully mined block.

4. Why do transaction fees change?

Bitcoin fees are influenced by supply and demand for block space.

When many people want to make transactions at the same time, competition for limited block space can increase.

When demand is lower, users may be able to get transactions confirmed with lower fee rates.

Network situation Possible effect
Low transaction demand Lower fee rates may be sufficient.
High transaction demand Users may compete with higher fee rates.
Many transactions waiting Some transactions may wait longer.

There is no single fixed Bitcoin transaction fee. The fee depends on the transaction and the conditions of the network.

5. What does sat/vB mean?

Wallets commonly show a fee rate measured in satoshis per virtual byte (sat/vB).

Let's break it down:

  • Satoshi: The smallest unit of bitcoin. One BTC equals 100 million satoshis.
  • Virtual byte (vB): A unit used to measure the size of a Bitcoin transaction for fee calculation.
  • sat/vB: The number of satoshis paid for each virtual byte.
Example calculation

Imagine a transaction has:

  • Transaction size: 200 vB
  • Fee rate: 10 sat/vB

Calculation:

200 × 10 = 2,000 satoshis

The transaction fee would be 2,000 satoshis, which equals 0.00002000 BTC.

This is an educational example, not a live fee recommendation.

6. Why does transaction size matter?

Bitcoin fees are generally calculated using transaction size and fee rate, not simply the amount of bitcoin being sent.

For example, sending 0.001 BTC does not automatically cost less than sending 1 BTC.

The transaction's size in virtual bytes and the chosen fee rate are important factors.

Imagine two transactions:

Transaction A sends 0.001 BTC and uses 200 vB.

Transaction B sends 1 BTC and also uses 200 vB.

If both pay 10 sat/vB, each pays a fee of 2,000 satoshis.

7. What is the mempool?

The mempool is a waiting area for unconfirmed transactions that individual Bitcoin nodes have received and accepted for relay.

When you broadcast a transaction, it may enter the mempool before a miner includes it in a block.

Miners can examine available transactions and choose which ones to include. Transactions offering higher fee rates may be more attractive, although miners can use different selection strategies.

Each node maintains its own mempool, so the contents are not necessarily identical everywhere.

8. Does paying a higher fee guarantee faster confirmation?

No. A higher fee rate can make a transaction more attractive to miners, but it does not guarantee confirmation in the next block or at an exact time.

Block production is not perfectly predictable, and miners make their own transaction-selection decisions.

Network demand can also change while your transaction is waiting.

Important

Do not assume that paying a very high fee guarantees an immediate confirmation.

9. How can you avoid overpaying?

  1. Use a reputable Bitcoin wallet.
  2. Check the wallet's fee estimate before approving a transaction.
  3. Understand the difference between a fee amount and a fee rate.
  4. Consider whether the payment needs to be confirmed quickly.
  5. Review the final fee shown by your wallet before signing.
  6. Learn whether your wallet supports fee replacement or other ways to handle a delayed transaction.

Do not blindly copy fee recommendations from random websites. Fee estimates change with network conditions.

10. What happens to fees over time?

Bitcoin's block subsidy is reduced through scheduled halvings. Transaction fees are a separate source of miner revenue.

As the subsidy decreases over many years, transaction fees are expected to play an increasingly important role in miner incentives.

How this develops will depend on future demand for block space, fees and the Bitcoin network's economic activity. The exact future cannot be guaranteed.

Final thought

Bitcoin transaction fees are part of how the network allocates limited block space and incentivises miners.

Understanding sat/vB, transaction size and the mempool helps you make more informed decisions when sending bitcoin.

Remember: verify the fee before approving your transaction, and never assume that a higher fee guarantees an exact confirmation time.
