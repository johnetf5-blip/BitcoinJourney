Lesson 19: Bitcoin's History and Satoshi Nakamoto

Bitcoin did not appear overnight. It came from decades of research into cryptography, digital money and computer networks. In this lesson, we will explore how Bitcoin began and how it developed into a global network.

1. The Financial Crisis of 2008

In 2008, the world experienced a major financial crisis. Banks failed or faced serious financial problems. Governments and central banks took emergency actions to support parts of the financial system.

The crisis increased public discussion about banks, debt, money and the financial system.

Bitcoin was proposed during this period as a peer-to-peer electronic payment system that could operate without requiring a financial institution to process payments.

Think about it: What if two people could send digital money directly to each other without needing a bank to approve the payment? This was one of the important ideas behind Bitcoin.

2. The Bitcoin White Paper

On 31 October 2008, someone using the name Satoshi Nakamoto published a document titled:

Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System

The document explained how a network of computers could record payments using cryptography, Proof of Work and a shared public transaction history.

The design aimed to allow online payments directly between people without relying on a central financial institution.

The white paper was only nine pages long, but it introduced the design that became Bitcoin.

3. Who Was Satoshi Nakamoto?

Satoshi Nakamoto is the name used by the person or people who created Bitcoin.

We know Satoshi communicated with other developers online, published the white paper and helped write the original Bitcoin software.

However, the real-world identity of Satoshi has never been reliably confirmed.

Important: Many people have been suggested as possible Satoshi Nakamoto. But no publicly verified evidence has proved who Satoshi really is.

4. The Genesis Block — 3 January 2009

On 3 January 2009, the first block of the Bitcoin blockchain was created. It is called the Genesis Block or Block 0.

The Genesis Block contained a reward of 50 BTC. This was the beginning of the Bitcoin blockchain.

It also contained a message referring to a newspaper headline from The Times of London:

"The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks"

The headline is often understood as a reference to the financial crisis and the banking system at that time.

5. The First Bitcoin Transaction

On 12 January 2009, Satoshi Nakamoto sent 10 BTC to computer developer Hal Finney.

This transaction is recorded in Block 170 and is recognised as the first Bitcoin transaction between two people.

Hal Finney was one of the earliest people to run the Bitcoin software and help test the network.

Why was this important? It demonstrated that Bitcoin could transfer value directly from one person to another using its network.

6. Bitcoin Pizza Day — 22 May 2010

One of the most famous events in Bitcoin history happened on 22 May 2010.

A programmer named Laszlo Hanyecz paid 10,000 BTC for two pizzas.

This event became known as Bitcoin Pizza Day. It is remembered as an important early example of Bitcoin being used to buy something in the real world.

Think about the lesson: At the time, Bitcoin was a very new experiment. People were discovering how it could be used for everyday payments.

7. Bitcoin's Early Development

Bitcoin did not become a global network in one day. Developers and users gradually helped improve the software, test the network and build tools around it.

  • Developers improved the open-source software.
  • More people downloaded and tested Bitcoin.
  • Exchanges and services began appearing.
  • Businesses and individuals experimented with payments.
  • More people began running their own Bitcoin nodes.

Bitcoin's development has continued through contributions from people around the world.

8. Why Did Satoshi Disappear?

Around 2010 and 2011, Satoshi gradually reduced involvement in the project.

By 2011, Satoshi had handed important development responsibilities to other contributors and stopped communicating regularly.

The exact personal reason for leaving is not known. We should be careful not to present guesses as facts.

Bitcoin continued operating and developing after Satoshi stopped participating.

A major idea: Bitcoin is open-source software. Its rules can be inspected, and people around the world can run their own nodes and independently verify the network.

9. Bitcoin History Timeline

3 January 2009

Genesis Block

12 January 2009

First Person-to-Person Transaction

Satoshi sends 10 BTC to Hal Finney.

22 May 2010

Bitcoin Pizza Day

Laszlo Hanyecz pays 10,000 BTC for two pizzas.

2011

Satoshi Steps Away

Satoshi stops participating regularly in Bitcoin development.
