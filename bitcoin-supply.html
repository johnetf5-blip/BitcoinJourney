Why Does Bitcoin Have a Maximum Supply of 21 Million?

One of Bitcoin's most important features is its limited supply. Unlike traditional money, Bitcoin follows a publicly verifiable issuance schedule designed around a maximum of approximately 21 million bitcoin.

The big number

21,000,000 BTC

This is the commonly stated maximum supply of Bitcoin. The actual total from the block reward schedule is slightly below 21 million because the rewards are rounded down to whole satoshis.

1. Why does Bitcoin have a fixed supply?

Bitcoin was designed with rules that control how new bitcoin enters circulation.

These rules are part of Bitcoin's protocol. They make its issuance schedule predictable and allow independent network participants to verify how much bitcoin has been issued.

Imagine a limited supply of gold.

Gold is naturally limited on Earth. It takes effort and resources to mine more.

Bitcoin is different because its scarcity comes from computer-enforced protocol rules rather than geology.

2. How are new bitcoins created?

New bitcoin enters circulation through mining. Miners use computing power to compete to add new blocks of transactions to the blockchain.

When a valid block is added, the miner can claim a block subsidy according to Bitcoin's rules. This subsidy creates new bitcoin.

Miners can also receive transaction fees. These fees are existing bitcoin paid by users, not newly created bitcoin.

3. What is Bitcoin halving?

Bitcoin's block subsidy is reduced by half every 210,000 blocks. This event is called a halving.

How the block reward changes

Period Block subsidy
2009 launch 50 BTC
2012 halving 25 BTC
2016 halving 12.5 BTC
2020 halving 6.25 BTC
2024 halving 3.125 BTC
Next halving 1.5625 BTC

The next halving occurs after another 210,000 blocks. Its exact date is not known in advance because block production times vary.

4. Why does the supply become harder to increase?

Every halving reduces the number of new bitcoins created through mining.

The subsidy becomes smaller and smaller. Eventually, it reaches zero through the protocol's reward schedule. Miners can then continue earning transaction fees.

A simple example

Imagine receiving 50 coins, then 25, then 12.5, then 6.25.

Each time, the amount becomes half of what it was before. Bitcoin uses this mathematical schedule to control the creation of new coins.

5. Can someone simply create more than 21 million?

Bitcoin's supply limit is enforced by the rules that validating nodes check.

A miner cannot make an invalid block acceptable simply by claiming a larger reward. Independent nodes reject blocks that break the consensus rules they follow.

Changing these rules would require broad acceptance across the Bitcoin network. It is not something one person or company can simply decide.
