Bitcoin vs Traditional Banking

How are banks and Bitcoin different? Who controls the money, how are payments processed, and who is responsible for protecting it?

1. How traditional banking works

Traditional banks are financial institutions that provide services such as holding deposits, processing payments and lending money.

When you deposit money into a bank account, the bank records the amount it owes you. Your account balance is an electronic record in the bank's database.

Banks can also create new deposit money when they make loans. This happens within banking regulations and other financial constraints.

Central banks influence the financial system through monetary policy, including interest rates and, in some circumstances, the creation of central bank money.

2. How Bitcoin works

Bitcoin is a decentralised digital monetary network. It does not depend on one bank or company to maintain its transaction history.

Transactions are broadcast to the network. Miners compete to add blocks using Proof of Work, while full nodes independently check that blocks and transactions follow the protocol's rules.

Bitcoin's issuance follows a published schedule. The block subsidy reduces through halvings, and the protocol's maximum supply is approximately 21 million bitcoin.

3. Comparing the two systems

Feature Traditional banking Bitcoin
Control Banks and financial authorities Decentralised network participants following consensus rules
Account records Maintained by banks Transactions recorded on the blockchain
Money creation Bank lending and central bank operations Protocol-defined block subsidy
Access Usually requires a bank account and provider approval Anyone can create a wallet, subject to local laws and practical access

Reversals Some payments can be reversed or disputed through providers Confirmed on-chain transactions are generally irreversible

4. Who controls your money?

With a bank: The bank maintains your account balance and processes your instructions. You depend on the bank to provide access to your account, subject to its rules and applicable law.

With self-custody Bitcoin: You hold the private keys that authorise spending. You are responsible for protecting your wallet and recovery phrase.

Self-custody gives you more direct control, but it also gives you more responsibility. If you lose your keys and have no usable backup, you may permanently lose access to your bitcoin.

5. Transparency and privacy

Bank account records are generally private between the customer and the financial institution, although information may be disclosed under legal or regulatory requirements.

Bitcoin transactions are recorded on a public blockchain. Anyone can inspect transaction data, but Bitcoin addresses do not automatically reveal the real-world identity of their owners.

Bitcoin is therefore transparent at the transaction level, but it is not automatically anonymous.

6. Are the two systems enemies?

Not necessarily. People and businesses may use both systems for different purposes.

Banks provide services such as loans, payment support and access to national currencies. Bitcoin offers a different way to hold and transfer value using a decentralised protocol.

Understanding their differences helps you make informed decisions. Neither system removes every financial risk.
