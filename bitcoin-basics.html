What Is Bitcoin?

Imagine being able to send money to someone in another country without asking a bank to approve the transaction. Bitcoin was designed to make this possible using a network of computers.

The simple definition

Bitcoin is a form of digital money. It operates on a network of computers around the world. No single company, bank, or government controls the Bitcoin network.

People can send and receive bitcoin over the internet, and the network records transactions on a shared public ledger called the blockchain.

1. Is Bitcoin physical money?

No. You cannot hold a bitcoin like a £10 banknote or a physical coin. Bitcoin exists digitally.

You can use a wallet to manage your bitcoin. A wallet helps you control the keys that authorise spending your bitcoin.

Example

Imagine you want to send £10 worth of bitcoin to a friend. Your wallet creates a transaction. The Bitcoin network checks it, and once confirmed, the transaction is recorded on the blockchain.

2. Who controls Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is a decentralised network. This means there is no single central organisation in charge of the entire system.

Thousands of independent computers, called nodes, can check that transactions and blocks follow Bitcoin's rules.

3. Why is Bitcoin different from ordinary money?

Traditional money: Most pounds and dollars are issued and managed through central banking systems.

Bitcoin: Its issuance follows rules built into its open-source protocol. Its maximum supply is designed to be 21 million bitcoin.

4. Can Bitcoin be sent anywhere?

Bitcoin can be sent across borders using an internet connection. Transactions are processed by the network rather than relying on one bank's payment system.

However, transactions are not always instant or free. Network fees and confirmation times can vary.

5. Is Bitcoin risk-free?

No. Bitcoin's price can rise and fall significantly. Losing your private keys or falling for a scam can also mean losing access to your bitcoin.

Learning how wallets, private keys, and backups work is essential before using Bitcoin.
