LESSON 17 · BITCOIN MINING

Bitcoin Mining Economics

Bitcoin mining is more than computers solving mathematical puzzles. It is an economic system that helps secure the Bitcoin network. But why do miners spend money on electricity and expensive machines? How do they earn Bitcoin?

1. What is Bitcoin mining?

Bitcoin mining is the process of using specialised computers to perform Proof of Work. Miners compete to find a valid block that follows Bitcoin's consensus rules.

Miners collect transactions, construct candidate blocks and repeatedly calculate hashes using the SHA-256 algorithm. They are searching for a hash that meets the network's current difficulty target.

Finding a valid block is difficult, but checking the result is relatively easy for other network participants.

TRANSACTIONS
↓
MINERS BUILD BLOCKS
↓
PROOF OF WORK
↓
FULL NODES VERIFY THE BLOCK

Important: miners produce candidate blocks, but full nodes independently verify whether those blocks follow Bitcoin's rules. Mining power alone cannot force a node to accept an invalid block.

2. How do miners earn Bitcoin?

Miners receive two main forms of revenue when they successfully produce a valid block:

  • Block subsidy: Newly issued bitcoin awarded to the miner who produces the valid block.
  • Transaction fees: Fees attached to the transactions included in that block.
Example: Block reward

Imagine a block contains:

Block subsidy: 3.125 BTC

Transaction fees: 0.15 BTC

Total block reward: 3.275 BTC

This is an illustrative example, not a guaranteed mining income.

Mining pools distribute earnings among participants according to their contribution and the pool's payment rules. Therefore, an individual miner may receive a share rather than the entire block reward.

3. Why does mining cost money?

Mining requires physical equipment and electricity. Running powerful machines continuously creates operating costs.

  • Electricity: Mining machines consume power around the clock.
  • Hardware: ASIC machines are specialised computers designed for Bitcoin mining.
  • Cooling: Equipment produces heat and may need ventilation or cooling systems.
  • Maintenance: Machines need repairs, replacement parts and monitoring.
  • Facilities: Buildings, internet connections and other infrastructure cost money.
Simple electricity example

Imagine a mining machine uses 3,000 watts of electricity.

That equals 3 kilowatts.

Running for 24 hours uses:

3 × 24 = 72 kWh per day.

At an electricity price of £0.20 per kWh:

72 × £0.20 = £14.40 per day.

This is only the electricity cost. It excludes equipment, cooling and other expenses.

4. What is mining difficulty?

Bitcoin automatically adjusts mining difficulty approximately every 2,016 blocks. At an average target of around 10 minutes per block, this is roughly every two weeks.

If blocks have been found too quickly, difficulty generally increases. If they have been found too slowly, difficulty generally decreases.

This adjustment helps Bitcoin maintain an average block production target of approximately 10 minutes over time. Individual blocks can take much less or much longer.

MORE MINING POWER
↓
DIFFICULTY ADJUSTMENT
↓
BLOCK TIME TARGET ≈ 10 MINUTES

Mining difficulty is not controlled by one company or government. It is calculated according to the rules followed by Bitcoin nodes.

5. How does the halving affect miners?

Approximately every 210,000 blocks, Bitcoin reduces the block subsidy by half. This is called the halving.

201225 BTC
201612.5 BTC
20206.25 BTC
20243.125 BTC
Next halving1.5625 BTC

When the subsidy falls, miners receive fewer newly issued bitcoins for each block. This can put pressure on miners whose operating costs are high.

Miners may respond by improving equipment efficiency, finding cheaper electricity or changing their operations.

Transaction fees can also become an increasingly important source of miner revenue as the block subsidy decreases.

6. What is mining profitability?

Mining profitability describes whether a miner earns more revenue than they spend on operating and maintaining equipment.

Basic calculation:

Total mining revenue: £30 per day

Electricity: £14.40 per day

Other operating costs: £5.60 per day

Estimated operating profit: £10 per day

This example assumes revenue is already converted into pounds. It does not include every possible business cost, taxes or equipment depreciation.

Real mining profitability changes with Bitcoin's market price, network difficulty, transaction fees, electricity prices, hardware efficiency and competition.

Mining is not guaranteed to make a profit.

7. Why is mining important to Bitcoin?

Mining provides Proof of Work, making it expensive to rewrite the blockchain's transaction history.

Miners compete to produce blocks. Full nodes independently verify the blocks, transactions and monetary rules.

The combination of mining incentives, cryptographic verification and independent node validation helps Bitcoin operate without requiring one central organisation to approve every transaction.

Mining does not give miners unlimited authority. A miner cannot create bitcoin beyond the permitted subsidy or make invalid transactions valid for nodes following the consensus rules.
