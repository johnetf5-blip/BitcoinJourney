Lesson 18: Bitcoin Security and Common Scams

Bitcoin gives people control over their own money. But with that control comes responsibility. If someone gets access to your private keys or recovery phrase, they may be able to steal your Bitcoin.

Important rule: Never share your Bitcoin recovery phrase or private keys with anyone. A legitimate support person does not need them.

1. What Are Private Keys?

A private key is a secret number that allows someone to spend Bitcoin from an address controlled by that key.

Think of your Bitcoin address like an account number that people can send money to. Your private key is like the secret authority that allows you to spend that money.

Example: You can give someone your Bitcoin address to receive Bitcoin. But you must never give them your private key.

2. Public Keys and Private Keys

Bitcoin uses cryptography to connect public keys and private keys.

  • Private key: Secret information used to authorise spending.
  • Public key: Information mathematically derived from a private key.
  • Bitcoin address: A payment destination derived from public-key information or a script.

You can share a Bitcoin address to receive payments. Keep your private keys secret.

3. What Is a Seed Phrase?

A seed phrase, also called a recovery phrase, is usually a list of 12 or 24 words used by a wallet to recover its keys.

If your hardware wallet breaks or you lose your device, the recovery phrase may allow you to restore access to your Bitcoin wallet.

Never do these things:
  • Send your seed phrase through WhatsApp or email.
  • Take a screenshot of your seed phrase.
  • Store it in an online document or cloud account.
  • Enter it into a website because someone tells you to.

4. Hardware Wallets and Software Wallets

Hardware wallets

A hardware wallet is a physical device designed to keep private keys protected from ordinary internet-connected computer environments.

Examples include Trezor and Ledger devices.

Software wallets

A software wallet is an application installed on a computer or mobile phone. It allows you to receive, store and send Bitcoin.

Software wallets can be convenient, but their security also depends on the security of the device they run on.

Remember: A hardware wallet does not remove every risk. You still need to protect your recovery phrase and verify what you approve on the device.

5. Common Bitcoin Scams

Fake investment promises

Someone promises to double your Bitcoin or guarantee enormous profits. These are warning signs of a scam.

Fake wallet applications

Criminals may create applications that look like real wallets. They may try to steal your recovery phrase.

Phishing attacks

A scammer sends a fake email, message or website pretending to be a wallet company or exchange.

Fake technical support

Someone contacts you claiming to help fix your wallet. They ask for your recovery phrase. This is a serious warning sign.

6. How to Protect Your Bitcoin

  • Buy hardware wallets from official or trusted sources.
  • Keep your recovery phrase offline and private.
  • Check website addresses carefully.
  • Download wallet software from official sources.
  • Verify the receiving address before sending Bitcoin.
  • Check transaction details on your hardware wallet screen.
  • Keep your computer and phone updated.
  • Be careful with unexpected messages and links.
  • Test a small transaction before sending a large amount.

7. Verify, Don't Trust

Bitcoin allows people to verify transactions and network rules instead of depending entirely on a bank or another person.

Running your own Bitcoin node allows you to independently check transactions and blocks according to the rules your node enforces.

Remember: a wallet helps you manage your keys, while a full node independently verifies the Bitcoin blockchain. They have different jobs.

Final lesson: Bitcoin gives you financial responsibility. Learn how your wallet works, protect your keys and never trust strangers with your recovery phrase.
