BITCOIN EDUCATION — LESSON 8

Bitcoin Wallets: Hot Wallets vs Cold Wallets

If you own bitcoin, you need a way to control it and authorise transactions.

Bitcoin wallets help you do this using cryptographic keys. But not all wallets work in the same way.

In this lesson, we will explore hot wallets, cold wallets, hardware wallets and recovery phrases.

1. What is a Bitcoin wallet?

A Bitcoin wallet is software or a device that helps you manage your Bitcoin keys and create transactions.

Your bitcoin is recorded on the Bitcoin blockchain. A wallet does not physically contain your bitcoin. It manages the keys that allow you to spend the bitcoin associated with your wallet.

Think of it this way:

Your bitcoin is recorded on the blockchain. Your wallet holds the tools needed to prove that you are authorised to spend it.

2. Public keys, private keys and addresses

Bitcoin uses cryptography to help control who can spend bitcoin.

  • Private key: A secret that allows the owner to authorise spending.
  • Public key: A cryptographic value derived from a private key.
  • Bitcoin address: A destination that someone can use to send bitcoin to you.

Remember this rule

You can share a Bitcoin receiving address when someone needs to pay you.

Never share your private keys or recovery phrase. Anyone who obtains them may be able to spend your bitcoin.

3. What is a hot wallet?

A hot wallet is a Bitcoin wallet that operates on an internet-connected device.

Examples include mobile wallets, desktop wallets and browser wallets.

Example:

You install a Bitcoin wallet on your smartphone. You can check your balance, receive payments and create transactions.

Advantages:

  • Convenient for everyday payments.
  • Usually quick to access.
  • Some options are free to use.

Risks:

  • Phones and computers can be infected with malware.
  • Fake wallet applications can steal information.
  • Phishing attacks can trick users into revealing secrets.

4. What is a cold wallet?

Cold storage means keeping private keys offline, away from an internet-connected environment.

Hardware wallets are a common way to use cold storage. They are dedicated devices designed to protect private keys and sign transactions.

Example:

You keep your private keys on a hardware wallet. When you want to send bitcoin, you prepare a transaction on your computer and approve it using the hardware device.

Advantages:

  • Private keys can remain isolated from your computer.
  • Useful for long-term self-custody.
  • Transactions can be reviewed and approved on the device.

Risks:

  • Devices can be lost, damaged or stolen.
  • A compromised recovery phrase can put funds at risk.
  • Users can still be tricked into approving malicious transactions.

5. Hot wallets vs cold wallets

Hot wallet

Connection: Online device

Best for: Everyday access

Example: Mobile wallet

Main concern: Online threats

Cold storage

Connection: Private keys kept offline

Best for: Longer-term storage

Example: Hardware wallet

Main concern: Backup and physical security

Neither option is automatically safe in every situation. Security also depends on how carefully the wallet is set up, backed up and used.

6. What is a recovery phrase?

Many Bitcoin wallets use a recovery phrase, commonly made up of 12 or 24 words.

It can help you recover access to your wallet if your device is lost or damaged, provided the wallet uses the same backup standard and any additional passphrase is also available.

Protect your recovery phrase

  • Write it down carefully and check the order.
  • Keep it somewhere private and secure.
  • Never photograph it or store it in ordinary cloud storage.
  • Never type it into a website or send it to someone.
  • Be careful of fake support staff asking for it.

Important: Never share your real recovery phrase with anyone, including me.

7. What happens if you lose your hardware wallet?

Losing a hardware wallet does not necessarily mean you have lost your bitcoin.

If you have a valid recovery backup and the necessary additional passphrase, you may be able to restore your wallet using a compatible wallet.

However, if someone obtains your recovery phrase, they may be able to restore your wallet and spend its bitcoin without possessing your original device.

Important distinction:

The hardware wallet protects access to your keys. The recovery backup helps you restore that access. Both need careful protection.

8. How do you choose a Bitcoin wallet?

Before choosing a wallet, consider these questions:

  1. Is it designed specifically for Bitcoin?
  2. Is the software or device from a trustworthy source?
  3. Can you independently verify receiving addresses?
  4. Do you understand how to back it up?
  5. Can you restore a wallet using a safe practice procedure?
  6. Does it allow you to control your own private keys?

Learn how a wallet works before sending a large amount of bitcoin to it. A small test transaction can help you understand the process.

Final thought

Bitcoin gives people the ability to control their own money, but that responsibility comes with the need to protect their keys.

A wallet is not just an application or a device. It is part of how you manage access to your bitcoin.

Understand your wallet, protect your backup and verify before approving transactions.
